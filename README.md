The computational prediction of protein-ligand binding is vital for drug discovery but remains hindered by the high cost of detailed physical simulations, creating a need for efficient pre-screening methods. This study posits that the geometric complementarity critical for binding can be modeled as a sphere packing problem, leading to the research question of whether the mathematics of Apollonian packings can provide a novel stability metric for docking. We hypothesized that the stability of a protein-ligand complex is proportional to the smallest eigenvalue of a generalized Curvature Matrix derived from the Descartes Circle Theorem. To test this, we first rigorously proved the matrix formulation for ideal packings and then conjectured its extension to non-ideal molecular configurations. The main method involved constructing this matrix for candidate docking poses and calculating its spectral properties. Our results confirmed the matrix properties for ideal packings and demonstrated computationally that the proposed stability metric successfully distinguishes between geometrically stable and unstable configurations in a model system. The key conclusion is that this spectral framework provides a mathematically grounded and computationally efficient tool for rapidly assessing geometric fit. The implication of these findings is the potential for a new class of ultra-fast docking filters that leverage geometric principles to prioritize candidates, thereby accelerating the early stages of drug discovery by reducing reliance on more expensive simulations.
